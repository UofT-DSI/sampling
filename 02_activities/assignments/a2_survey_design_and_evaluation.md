## Part B - Survey Evaluation:

Identify and describe survey features:

```
1. Sample type: stratified simple random sample without replacement (stratification was by province)
2. Sample size: 20,000 was the targeted sample size
3. Target population: All persons 15 years of age and older in Canada, excluding residents of Yukon, Northwest Territories, and Nunavut and full-time residents of instituitions.
4. Sampling frame: Telephone numbers Statistics Canada had access to from various sources and the address register for all dwellings living in the 10 provinces were used to create "records" consisting of either (1) all telephone numbers linked to an address register entry (86% of telephone numbers were linked) and (2) telephone numbers not linked to address register entrys (14% of telephone numbers). These two type of records resulted in the survey frame.
5. Survey mode(s): Data were collected electronically as a self-completed questionnaire (rEQ) as well as via computer assisted telephone interviews (iEQ).
6. Timeline: Data were collected between September to December, 2018
7. Response rate: 41.9%
8. Weights: Survey estimates were adjusted (weighted) to represent all persons in the target population, including those not covered by the survey frame. The weights were adjusted for differences in selection, non-response, income, age, and sex and possible other factors to obtain weights that would reflect the target population when applied. 
9. Data processing: Data captured via the online survey was entered directly into a program that had built in edits and following the flow of the questionnaire. Data captured via interview was also entered into the program by the interviewer. For questions with write-in responses, the responses were coded into categories, when possible using standard classification systems. 
10. Cleaning, imputation, etc: Verification was performed to identify and remove duplicate records and drop non-response and other of scope records. Missing or incorrect survey responses were corrected or filled in using other questionnaire information when possible. Questions that applied to respondents were kept or marked as “Not Stated” if unanswered, while questions that did not apply to a respondent were coded as “Valid Skip.” The survey handled different types of non-response in several ways. Complete non-response records were removed and adjusted for through weighting. Item non-response was handled by assigning the “not stated” code or imputation. Partial non-response was handled by donor imputation to fill in some missing responses. The survey mainly used donor imputation to fill in missing responses by matching respondents with similar completed records. When donor imputation was not possible, mean imputation was used.
11. Sources of error: Possible sources of error include sampling error (unavoidable in surveys) and non-sampling error (e.g., interviewers misunderstand instructions, respondents make an error in answering the questions, incorrect inputting of answers into the system, non-response, ...).
12. Limitations, known biases, etc: Althought Statistics Canada has done their best to mitigate the impact of bias (e.g., through weighting), the survey could be affected by many sources of bias including non-response bias, coverage bias, recall bias, and social desirability bias. Other limitations of the survey could be that it doesnt include residents of Yukon, Northwest Territories, and Nunavut, which is a common limitation of Statistics Canada national surveys.
13. Link to documentation and any additional sources used: I used the data available in the downloadable survey PUMF, specifically the PUMF User Guide and the data dictionary (https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/45-25-0001/cat5/c33_2018.zip)

```
