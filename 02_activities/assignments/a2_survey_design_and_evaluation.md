Describe your target population, sampling frame, sampling units, and observational units:
```
Target population: Entry and lower level employees at the company, including both current employees and employees who left within the past six months.

Sampling frame: The company HR database for current employee records and recent departing records for entry and lower level positions.

Sampling units: Individual current employees and recently departed employees selected from the HR database.

Observational units: Individual employees or former employees, since each survey response describes one person's workplace experience.

Sampling strategy: I would use stratified random sampling. Employees would be stratified by department, employment status (current vs. recently departed), and job level. This strategy ensures that departments and employee groups with different turnover experiences are represented. Because the company is specifically concerned about entry- and lower-level turnover, those groups would be the focus of the sample, with possible oversampling of departments that have especially high turnover.
```

## Part B - Survey Evaluation:

Identify and describe survey features:

```
Survey evaluated: Canadian General Social Survey on Giving, Volunteering, and Participating, 2018 (Cycle 33), conducted by Statistics Canada.

1. Sample type
The survey used a cross-sectional sample survey design. It was based on a stratified probability sample, with stratification at the province/census metropolitan area level. The survey also used a two-stage design: groups of telephone numbers or linked telephone/address records were sampled first, and one eligible person aged 15 or older was randomly selected within each sampled household. Statistics Canada also used rejective sampling/subsampling for some non-volunteers, so that volunteers received the long interview and some non-volunteers received either a long or short interview.

2. Sample size
Statistics Canada reported a field sample of approximately 50,000 units. About 40,000 invitation letters to the electronic questionnaire were sent to selected households, and about 24,000 completed questionnaires were expected.

3. Target population
The target population was persons aged 15 years and older living in Ontario and/or the ten provinces of Canada. The survey excluded residents of the territories and full-time residents of institutions.

4. Sampling frame
The sampling frame combined landline and cellular telephone numbers from the Census and administrative sources with Statistics Canada's dwelling frame. Records were groups of one or more telephone numbers associated with the same address, or a single telephone number when a telephone/address link could not be established.

5. Survey mode(s)
Data were collected directly from respondents through an electronic questionnaire or through computer-assisted telephone interviewing (CATI). Respondents could answer in English, French, and other commonly spoken languages in Ontario/Canada (such as Mandarin Chinese; Spanish; Punjabi; etc.)

6. Timeline
The reference period was the past 12 months before the interview date. Data collection for the 2018 cycle occurred from September 4, 2018 to December 28, 2018. The data were released on January 26, 2021.

7. Weights
The main person-level weight was WGHT_PER, which allows analysts to produce estimates for the non-institutionalized population aged 15 and older. The weights account for the probability of selection, the rejective sampling/subsampling design, and non-response adjustments. Bootstrap weights were also created for design-based variance estimation.

8. Data processing
Statistics Canada processed the data using generalized processing steps and utilities. Processing included automated and manual edits at macro and micro levels, including family, consistency, and flow edits. CATI programming also included range checks and questionnaire-flow controls during data capture.

9. Cleaning, imputation, etc.
Statistics Canada used editing and imputation to handle incomplete or inconsistent responses. Most imputations used donor imputation, where a similar donor record was selected through a score function. When donor imputation could not be used, mean imputation from a donor pool was used. Imputation was applied to income, formal volunteering, informal volunteering, donation variables, and solicitation-method variables. Income information was also improved by linking survey responses to tax data when respondents did not object.

11. Sources of error
The survey was subject to sampling error because estimates were based on a sample rather than a census. It was also subject to non-sampling error, including coverage error, non-response error, response error, and processing error. Sampling variability can be estimated using the bootstrap weights.

12. Limitations, known biases, etc.
The survey excluded full-time residents of institutions, residents of the territories, households without telephones, and households with telephone services not covered by the frame. These exclusions may create coverage bias if excluded groups differ from the surveyed population. Non-response bias is also possible, although Statistics Canada adjusted weights to account for non-response and used administrative information such as income and household composition to improve non-response adjustment. Another limitation is that 2018 estimates should be compared carefully with earlier GSS GVP cycles because the 2018 survey added an electronic questionnaire option and underwent methodological and content changes.

13. Link to documentation and sources used
Sources used: Statistics Canada’s 2018 GSS GVP survey-methodology page, the Statistics Canada PUMF documentation/user-guide catalogue page, and the Statistics Canada release page for the 2018 GSS GVP public-use microdata file. These sources provide the official documentation for methodology, target population, sampling, data collection, response rate, weights, processing, imputation, and known limitations.

```
